## Introduction requirements (announcement, photo 01)

The introduction should include:

- a) A background review of the state of the art in the relevant field.
- b) The problem addressed in the project.
- c) Objective of the proposed project.
- d) The adopted approach.
- e) A discussion of the current trends in the relevant field.
- f) Real-world commercial systems (or prototypes) related to the project, addressing the current level of research and development, ethics, economics and sustainability.
- g) A literature review with formal citations in the Reference Section (Section 12).

**Length and content: at least 5 pages of text, 3–5 figures, 1–2 tables, and references.**

## What the sample introduction does (photos 02–13)

The sample is an elderly-care storage and retrieval robot. The whole document is 7 pages and about 3,300 words.

**Structure:**
1. **Opens with the societal problem and a statistic** (photo 02): "In the next 50 years, there stands to be a major demographic shift in the United States…", citing the Population Reference Bureau [1]. It ends the opening paragraph by naming the societal issue the team addresses.
2. **Backs the problem with official data** (photo 03): quotes Census Bureau projections (65+ population from 52 million in 2018 to 95 million by 2060), then lists the risks the population faces.
3. **Figure from an authoritative source** (photo 04): a U.S. Census Bureau population-pyramid chart, "From Pyramid to Pillar: A Century of Change".
4. **Commercial product with a photo** (photos 05–07): describes ElliQ, an existing elder-care robot, with a product photo. The caption describes the product and gives its source URL (IEEE Spectrum).
5. **Their solution** (photos 07–08): "Our solution is…", then how it addresses the problem, why the design is simple (target users are less comfortable with technology) and how the robot works step by step.
6. **Design details and features** (photo 09): hardware choices (stepper motor, Arduino, IR sensors), a first prototype and why it changed, and the main features.
7. **References** (photos 10–13): "References (to be moved in combined References later)", numbered IEEE-style entries with [Online], Available: URL and [Accessed: date]. The sources are mostly government and organizational (Census, CDC, NIH, Alzheimer's Association) plus journal articles.

**Formatting:** Times New Roman, single-spaced, justified text; figure captions start with a bold "Figure N."

## Takeaways for our introduction

- **Length:** at least 5 pages of text, not counting figures and tables. The sample is single-spaced.
- **Figures:** the sample uses real images with sources (an official data chart and a real product photo), not generic diagrams. Ours should follow that pattern: real statistics, real products or prototypes, and our own design.
- **Tables:** 1–2 are required.
- **Opening:** start from the societal need and back it with statistics from authoritative sources.
- **References:** list them after the introduction for now, noting they'll be moved to Section 12. Include access dates for online sources.
